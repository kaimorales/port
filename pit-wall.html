What’s under the hood?

A little exercise in how a good decision depends on its assumptions. Both cars get the same forecast, the same pace model, and exactly one stop. The orange car exhaustively checks all 368 combinations of tire choices and pit laps. It knows when the rain will arrive.

Fresh softs are quickest; wear grows with the square of tire age. Hard tires trade speed for durability. Rain builds over three laps, slows slicks, and makes intermediates useful. A stop costs 20 seconds. Tires change after that lap. There’s no overtaking penalty, traffic, safety car, randomness, or live racing data.

This is a deliberately simplified model, not an F1 prediction. “Optimal” means fastest within these rules. Change the forecast and the answer can change.
